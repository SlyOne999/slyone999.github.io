## Description ##
The Screen Crab is a covert video man-in-the-middle (MitM) device designed for physical penetration testing, security auditing, and surveillance. Developed by Hak5, it sits inline between a computer (or any video source) and its monitor, allowing an operator to secretly capture screenshots or stream full video over the air.🛠️ Core CapabilitiesThe device is engineered to remain completely invisible to the host computer's operating system, exploiting physical hardware video connections rather than software:Inline Video Interception: Connects via dual HDMI ports (In and Out), sitting silently between a device (like a desktop PC, server, or gaming console) and its display monitor without causing lag or screen flicker.Onboard Wi-Fi Connectivity: Features a built-in wireless chip, enabling the operator to remotely configure settings, download captured images, or stream live video from a smartphone or laptop.MicroSD Storage: Features a hidden card slot to securely log screenshots or full-motion video locally if wireless access is restricted.Cloud C2 Integration: Connects natively to Hak5’s central Command and Control platform, allowing an auditor to monitor a target's screen from anywhere in the world over the internet.Hardware Invisibility: Because it operates strictly on the physical video signal, it requires no software drivers and cannot be detected by antivirus software, firewalls, or task managers.📡 Primary Types of Attacks1. Visual Credential SniffingPassword Harvesting: Capturing screenshots at regular intervals (e.g., every 5 seconds). When a user types their password into a field and accidentally clicks the "reveal password" eye icon, or inputs sensitive corporate keys, the device captures the plaintext text visually.Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) Bypass: Visually intercepting temporary 2FA codes, backup keys, or QR authorization codes as they appear on the victim's screen.2. Persistent Visual EspionageData Exfiltration Monitoring: Watching what documents an employee opens, reading confidential emails as they write them, or viewing sensitive financial spreadsheets that never leave the local network.Server Room Surveillance: Plugging inline behind a critical data-centre server console terminal to monitor exactly what maintenance commands or administrative changes are being made to infrastructure.3. Social Engineering & Covert PlacementThe "Cable Clutter" Disguise: The device is small and looks like a generic video adapter or signal booster. An attacker can easily tuck it away out of sight amidst the tangled cable nests behind office desks or server racks.
